An error with a direction is a missing term, not noise — and the term it is missing is one the lap times cannot measure, because teams stop before it bites.

What a pit stop costs

The feed records a pit-lane transit time, not a pit loss. The cost is measured instead as the excess of the in-lap and the out-lap over what the car's own fitted line says those laps should have taken: 21.75 s under green flag across 505 stops, interquartile 19.75 to 23.84. The same measurement over the 174 stops taken while the race was neutralised gives 7.43 s, a discount of 0.34×. That discount is the only reason a strategy model needs a simulation at all.

The race as arithmetic

Race time splits into a part no plan can change and a part that is entirely the plan: the sum of squared stint lengths, and the stops. Two things follow without simulating anything. Equal stints minimise the sum of squares, so the best plan at any stop count is the balanced split. And differentiating gives the best number of stints outright, k* = N√(d/2L), which at the pooled numbers over 57 laps is 1.537.

Why that result is unusable. Teams choose when to stop, and they stop before the tyre falls away. So a stint only reaches a high age when that particular set happened to be holding on — which is selection directly on the thing being measured. The laps that would reveal a cliff are the laps strategy exists to avoid.

That argument can be tested where the truth is known. Below, every car is given the same real cliff of +0.0800 s per lap beyond age 20, and the two arms differ only in how stints end.
